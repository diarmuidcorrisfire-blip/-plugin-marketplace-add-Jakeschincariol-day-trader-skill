# Overnight strategy research — Fri Nov 27 2026 (pretend money only)

## What ran
- **14,417,568 stock × strategy tests**: 1,920 rule variants (dip-buying, breakouts, big-down-day
  bounces; different exits, holding periods and stop-losses) on **5,246 US stocks and 907 European
  stocks**, 10 years of daily prices, Trading 212 fees included.
- Rules **chosen on 2016–2021**, **judged on 2022–2026** (includes the 2022 bear market).
- Day-after-Thanksgiving study (2017–2025), Congress trades (16 filings), overnight Nvidia news.

## What we learned
1. **Nothing is guaranteed, and most "great" rules are luck.** The rules that looked best on
   2016–2021 (breakouts) made money in only about half of the 31-day periods afterwards, with worst
   months of −8% to −20% of the money in the portfolio.
2. **Yesterday's dip-buy winner fails on all 907 European stocks** (≈ +0.07% a trade, losing years
   2018, 2020, 2022) and **loses on US stocks** (FX fee plus no edge).
3. **On the 39 big European stocks it still works modestly over 10 years:**

| 39 big EU stocks, 3 positions | 2017–2026 |
|---|---|
| Rule | Buy above 200-day avg when RSI(3) < 15; sell above 3-day avg or after 5 days |
| Average trade | +0.18% (1,660 trades, 65% winners) |
| Years profitable | 9 of 10 (2020 lost) |
| 31-day periods profitable | 318 of 478 (67%) |
| Worst 31 days | about −20% of the money in it |
| Average return | about 6% a year — similar to simply holding an index fund, with more crash risk |

   Caveat: these 39 are today's big companies (survivorship), which flatters the past.

4. **Black Friday half days (2017–2025):** the average US stock rose from open to close in 6 of 9
   years (+0.2%). Stocks opening >1% higher kept rising in 8 of 9 years (+0.7% average, about +0.4%
   after Trading 212's FX fee). Only 9 data points.

## Recommendation
- Keep everything on pretend money. The 39-stock dip-buy paper book keeps running daily.
- There is no strategy here that reliably turns small money into big money in 31 days.
- For your long-term money, a low-cost index fund historically does about as well as the best rule
  found tonight, with far less work and risk.
